# Clear Language Creates Better Decisions

Clear language is not cosmetic.

It creates better decisions.

In business, vague language creates vague action. When people say, “We need to improve the process,” “The system is not working,” “The reporting needs to be better,” or “We need more alignment,” the statements may be true, but they are not precise enough to drive execution.

A useful advisory frame is:

> Name it. Size it. Own it. Decide it.

Name the issue clearly. Size the impact. Assign ownership. Identify the decision required.

For example, instead of saying:

> “The process is broken.”

A clearer statement is:

> “The intake-to-resolution process has unclear ownership between triage and fulfillment, which is causing delayed handoffs and inconsistent status updates. We need to confirm the accountable owner and escalation rule.”

That version creates action.

MECE thinking helps improve language because it forces structure. Instead of broad statements, break the issue into distinct components. Is the problem related to process design, role clarity, data quality, technology capability, policy, governance, or adoption?

Lean Six Sigma also values precise problem statements. A good problem statement identifies what is happening, where it is happening, when it happens, the size of the impact, and why it matters. It avoids jumping to solutions too early.

For example, “Customers are unhappy” is not actionable. “Customer complaints related to transfer delays increased by 18% over the last quarter, primarily in the billing queue, due to unclear routing and escalation rules” is actionable.

TOGAF also depends on clear language. Business capabilities, requirements, architecture principles, constraints, and transition states must be defined clearly to avoid misinterpretation across teams.

A useful advisory phrase is:

> “Let’s be specific: what is the issue, why does it matter, who owns it, and what decision is needed?”

Clear language reduces ambiguity. It improves stakeholder confidence. It accelerates decisions. It reduces rework. It helps executives understand the real trade-offs.

In advisory work, clarity is not about sounding sophisticated. It is about making the work executable.

**Advisor takeaway:** The clearer the language, the better the decision. Precision is not style; it is a leadership tool.
