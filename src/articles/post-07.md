# Training Is Not the Finish Line

Training is often treated as the final step before go-live.

The session is delivered. The slide deck is shared. Attendance is captured. The project status turns green.

But training is not the finish line. Training is the beginning of adoption.

In transformation work, the real question is not whether training happened. The real question is whether people can perform the new behavior confidently in the live environment.

A practical adoption frame is:

> Teach it. Practice it. Support it. Reinforce it.

This is where many programs fall short. They focus heavily on knowledge transfer but not enough on practice, role-based scenarios, coaching, job aids, supervisor reinforcement, support channels, and post-launch feedback loops.

ADKAR is a useful framework here. Training often addresses Knowledge, but adoption also requires Awareness, Desire, Ability, and Reinforcement. A person may know what is changing but still not be ready, willing, or able to perform differently.

For example, in a contact center migration, agents may need to learn a new desktop, new call controls, new queue behavior, new dispositions, new escalation paths, new knowledge tools, and new reporting expectations. Supervisors may need to interpret new dashboards, coach new behaviors, and support agents through early stabilization.

One generic training session is rarely enough.

A stronger advisory approach is to design training around job performance. What must an agent do on day one? What must a supervisor monitor? What exceptions are likely? What job aids are needed? What does success look like after two weeks?

Lean thinking also applies. Training should reduce process waste, rework, handoff errors, and avoidable escalations. If training does not improve flow and performance, it is incomplete.

A practical example:

Instead of training agents only on a platform feature, train them on real scenarios:

* How to receive a call
* How to authenticate the customer
* How to transfer correctly
* How to disposition the interaction
* How to escalate
* What to do when the expected workflow fails
* Where to get help

This moves training from tool orientation to operational readiness.

A useful advisory line is:

> “We should not measure training by whether it was delivered. We should measure whether people can perform the new behavior in the live environment.”

That statement shifts the conversation from completion to adoption.

**Advisor takeaway:** Training is delivered once. Adoption must be reinforced. The business value is not in the session; it is in the sustained behavior after launch.
