# The Meeting Is Not the Outcome

A meeting is not an outcome.

A meeting is a mechanism. Its value depends on what changes because it happened.

Many organizations confuse governance activity with governance effectiveness. They have recurring meetings, status reports, steering committees, working groups, dashboards, and action logs. Yet the same blockers keep appearing.

That is a sign that the governance system is active but not effective.

A useful advisory frame is:

> If nothing moved, it was just a conversation.

A productive meeting should do at least one of four things:

* Make a decision
* Reduce a risk
* Resolve a dependency
* Clarify ownership

If none of those happened, the meeting may have created awareness, but not progress.

This is especially important in transformation programs where time is expensive and stakeholder attention is limited. Senior leaders should not be asked to attend meetings that do not require a decision, escalation, or strategic direction.

Lean principles are relevant here. Meetings can become waste if they do not improve flow or remove blockers. In Lean terms, unnecessary meetings are a form of overprocessing.

A practical improvement is to design meetings by purpose:

* Decision meetings
* Risk review meetings
* Delivery coordination meetings
* Executive steering meetings
* Design workshops
* Operational readiness reviews

Each forum should have a clear purpose, inputs, decision rights, and expected outputs.

For example, a weekly project meeting should not become a general discussion forum. It should review milestones, risks, dependencies, decisions needed, and actions due. A steering committee should focus on decisions and escalations, not detailed task updates.

A useful advisory line is:

> “Before we close, what decision moved, what risk reduced, or what action now has a clear owner?”

That question brings discipline to governance.

OKRs can also help. If meetings are not advancing objectives or key results, the governance rhythm should be reconsidered.

Good advisors do not only attend meetings. They shape meetings so that leadership time is converted into decisions and delivery movement.

**Advisor takeaway:** The value of a meeting is not that it happened. The value is what changed because it happened.
